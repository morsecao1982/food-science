🍦 Lab Task: Ice Cream Formation

Use the tournament method as the starting protocol; plan fair trials by changing one variable at a time.

Safety and rule check: An adult supervises home trials. Wear the tournament's required goggles and protective clothing. Avoid tasting experimental samples. Before competition, confirm current amounts and scoring in the official rules manual.

Why this works

The outer bag is a cold bath. Salt dissolves in water on melting ice and lowers its freezing point. Melting ice absorbs heat from the dairy mixture. Shaking improves contact between the mix and cold bag wall. Sugar and other dissolved particles also affect how the mix freezes; fat, proteins, ice crystals, and air affect texture and shape.

Rule-based starting procedure

  1. Measure up to 125 mL of milk, cream, or half-and-half into the small/pint zip bag.
  2. Add no more than 15 mL sugar and up to 1.2 mL vanilla, according to the rule's allowed quantities and your experimental design. Seal carefully and record the exact amounts.
  3. Put the small bag inside the gallon bag. Add ice to about half-fill the outer bag and add up to 90 mL salt, following the manual.
  4. Seal the gallon bag. Shake for 5–10 minutes using a consistent motion and time it.
  5. At the assigned end point, form the ice cream on the surface in the manner required by the rules. Measure height after it stands by itself for 5 seconds (or the exact current rule interval).
  6. Record the raw measurement, units, start/end times, observations, and any leaks or unusual conditions.

Design three distinct notebook experiments

Each entry changes one independent variable. A reasonable sequence could be (1) outer-bath salt quantity, (2) sugar amount, and (3) dairy base. For each, choose a few levels that are safe and within rules, and use the same standard recipe for all non-tested factors. You may choose a different sequence; explain why it answers a clear question.

What to calculate and explain

  • Keep each raw measurement; if repeats are allowed, calculate the mean as (sum of measurements) ÷ number of trials.
  • Graph the independent variable against the measured height; include units on both axes.
  • Explain the pattern using heat flow and freezing point depression. Avoid claiming “salt creates cold.”
  • Report unexpected results honestly and identify plausible errors (leaky bag, uneven shaking, different mix start temperature, inaccurate volume, inconsistent height test).
