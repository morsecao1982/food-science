📚 Food Science: the complete course

Start with milk as a system, then learn how its molecules behave, how dairy foods are made, what labels tell you, and how to plan experiments. Every rule topic is taught here in student-friendly language.

How to use these lessons

Read one lesson, explain its main idea out loud, then answer the “check yourself” questions without looking. Follow the ten-week plan for spacing and lab deadlines. “PDF page” below means the page number in the supplied local file On Food and Cooking _ The Science and Lore of the Kitchen.pdf; chapter headings are included so you can find the same passage in a different edition.

These are original explanations and summaries, not a reproduction of the book. The book is a source to consult for the cited topics; official rule wording and current government guidance take priority where a number or regulation can change.

Lesson 1

Milk is a molecular system

Composition, water, lactose, fat globules, proteins, minerals, dairy history, and why milk is an emulsion plus a colloid plus a solution.

 Lesson 2

Sugars, fats & freezing

Carbohydrate and lipid structures, triglycerides, saturation, essential fats, ester bonds, emulsifiers, freezing point depression, and ice cream texture.

 Lesson 3

Proteins, curds & cultures

Casein micelles, whey, acid and enzyme coagulation, cooking chemistry, curd yield, fermentation, probiotics, and food safety.

 Lesson 4

Processing, labels & food choices

Pasteurization, homogenization, preservation, lactose-free and non-dairy drinks, allergens, organic claims, and label math.

 Lesson 5

Dairy nutrition & deficiencies

What dairy contributes, essential nutrients, age-specific recommended amounts, deficiency effects, %DV, and careful interpretation of health claims.

 Lesson 6

Think like a food scientist

Independent variables, fair tests, data tables, calculations, interpreting station evidence, and the three tournament lab tasks.
