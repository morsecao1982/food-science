📅 Ten-week study plan

September 28 – December 4, 2026 · Invitational: Saturday, December 5. Learn from the course pages first; linked books and government pages are source references and optional deeper reading.

Weekly rhythm

Plan four short study sessions each week: (1) read the lesson and write a one-paragraph summary; (2) make flashcards or teach the idea out loud; (3) work on one experiment entry or lab practice; (4) answer review questions without notes. Keep the experiment notebook moving from Week 1, because at least three trials for each of the three tasks are required before impound under the rules described here.

Course coverage: lessons contain the actual explanations and exam-ready facts. Guelph is the technical dairy-science reference; NIH supplies nutrient/RDA tables; FDA explains labels and allergens; USDA AMS explains organic categories. Read the on-page explanations first so studying does not depend on opening external sites.

Week 1 · Sep 28–Oct 4 FoundationsMilk system
What milk contains and how its phases are organized
  • Study water, lactose, milkfat, casein, whey, and minerals.
  • Distinguish solution, colloid, and emulsion; explain milk as all three.
  • Learn cream separation and why homogenization reduces it.
  • Make an “ingredient → structure → food behavior” concept map.
Notebook checkpoint: choose an ice-cream question; draft trial 1 before testing.

Week 2 · Oct 5–11 Ice CreamCarbs, lipids, freezing
From lactose and fat chemistry to freezing point depression
  • Sketch lactose from glucose + galactose; learn mono-, di-, and polysaccharides.
  • Sketch glycerol + 3 fatty acids → triglyceride + water; identify ester bonds.
  • Compare saturated and unsaturated fatty acids; know linoleic and alpha-linolenic acids as essential fats.
  • Explain why salt lets a melting ice bath get colder and how heat leaves the mix.
  • Describe ice crystals, concentrated liquid, and air cells in ice cream.
Notebook: run and record ice-cream experiment 1. Change only one factor.

External source map

University of Guelph Dairy Science and Technology eBook: in-depth explanations of milk composition, chemistry, processing, cultured products, butter, and ice cream. The course lessons summarize the portions relevant to this event.

NIH Office of Dietary Supplements: current role, intake, food source, and deficiency information for calcium, vitamin D, riboflavin, B12, and other vitamins/minerals. Lesson 5 includes middle-school-age amounts.

FDA: Nutrition Facts %DV and major allergen labeling information. Lesson 4 explains how to read the label and distinguish milk protein allergy from lactose intolerance.

USDA Agricultural Marketing Service: the organic label categories and seal rules. Lesson 4 summarizes the categories for packaged products.
